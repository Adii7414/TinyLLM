This small sample is included so the entire educational language-model pipeline
can be tested immediately. A useful model needs much more text, and the model
will learn patterns from the exact material you provide.

Airplanes are machines that fly through the atmosphere. An aircraft generates
lift with its wings, moves forward with thrust, and uses control surfaces to
change direction. Pilots monitor speed, altitude, weather, fuel, navigation,
and the health of the aircraft systems.

The Airbus A321neo is a narrow-body commercial jet. The word neo means new
engine option. Airlines use aircraft such as the A321neo to connect cities
efficiently. A safe flight begins with careful planning, a preflight check,
clear communication, and respect for operating limits.

A language model reads a sequence of byte tokens and predicts what byte comes
next. During training it compares its prediction with the actual next byte.
With enough varied examples, the model can learn spelling, punctuation, facts,
and the style of the training text. A tiny model is useful for learning how
the mechanism works, but it does not have the broad knowledge of a large
production system.

Transformer attention lets each position look at earlier positions. A causal
mask hides future positions, because a next-token predictor must not receive
the answer before it makes a prediction. Repeated practice updates randomly
initialized weights with gradients so that useful patterns become more likely.

User: Tell me about airplanes
Assistant: Airplanes use wings to create lift and engines to create thrust.
User: What is a byte?
Assistant: A byte is a group of eight bits and can represent 256 values.
